# Management brief

## Headline

Community mental-health contact rates increased across Gippsland, Murray and Western Victoria between 2018–19 and 2023–24. Acute-care rates were comparatively stable over the five-year period, but ED and hospitalisation rates grew faster than community contacts in every PHN in the latest year.

## Evidence

- Community-contact rates increased by 20.2% in Gippsland, 36.1% in Murray and 30.0% in Western Victoria.
- ED presentation rates were lower in 2023–24 than in 2018–19 in every PHN. Hospitalisation rates increased by between 1.5% and 8.1%.
- Between 2022–23 and 2023–24, Western Victoria's ED presentation rate increased 29.0%, compared with 9.5% growth in community contacts.
- The regional range for community-contact rates widened from 309 to 933 contacts per 10,000. ED and hospitalisation ranges narrowed slightly.
- In 2023–24, Murray recorded the highest community-contact and ED rates; Gippsland recorded the highest hospitalisation rate.

## Operational response

Monitor whether the latest acute-care increase persists in more recent data. Prioritise review of Western Victoria's ED change and Gippsland's hospitalisation profile. Test whether changes in demand, acuity, access, capacity, referral pathways, workforce, coding or collection coverage explain the observations. Use patient-flow and outcome data before deciding whether a service response is required.

## Interpretation boundary

These are crude activity rates. They do not measure performance quality, unmet need or outcomes, and they cannot show that changes in one care setting caused changes in another.
