Victorian regional mental-health service utilisation
Sagrika Chugh

Business question: How has mental-health service utilisation changed across regional Victorian PHNs, and which patterns warrant further operational review?

This dashboard summarises published all-age rates for Gippsland, Murray and Western Victoria from 2018–19 to 2023–24. It is designed for descriptive performance reporting and monitoring.

Interpretation boundary: Activity rates describe service utilisation. They do not measure quality, unmet need or outcomes, and they cannot establish that changes in one care setting caused changes in another.

What the evidence shows
  • Community-contact rates increased across all three PHNs. Five-year growth ranged from 20.2% in Gippsland to 36.1% in Murray.
  • Acute-care rates were comparatively stable over five years. ED presentation rates were lower in 2023–24 than in 2018–19 in every PHN; hospitalisation rates increased by between 1.5% and 8.1%.
  • The latest year differed from the longer trend. ED and hospitalisation rates grew faster than community contacts in every PHN between 2022–23 and 2023–24.
  • Regional profiles differ. Murray recorded the highest latest community-contact and ED rates, while Gippsland recorded the highest hospitalisation rate.
  • The community-contact range widened. The gap between the highest and lowest PHN rates increased from 309 to 933 contacts per 10,000.
Why this warrants monitoring

The latest increase in acute activity warrants monitoring because it differs from the broader five-year pattern.

Priority questions are whether demand, clinical acuity, access, capacity, referral pathways, workforce, coding or collection coverage changed in 2023–24.

Operational data should be reviewed before deciding whether a service response is required.

How has the service mix changed?

Management takeaway: Community activity increased more than ED presentations or hospitalisations over the full period in every PHN.

Which indicators changed most?

Management takeaway: Murray recorded the largest community-contact increase; Gippsland recorded the largest ED decrease; Western Victoria recorded the largest hospitalisation increase.

Which PHNs recorded the highest latest rates?

Management takeaway: A higher rate does not by itself indicate better or poorer performance. Interpret the level alongside population need, access, service models and data coverage.

Suggested operational questions
  • Did demand, case complexity or clinical acuity change in 2023–24?
  • Were there changes in access, waiting times, service capacity, opening hours or referral pathways?
  • Did hospital or community-service coding, coverage or reporting practices change?
  • Are the trends concentrated in particular age groups, local areas, providers or diagnostic groups?
  • Do patient-flow data show repeated ED use, readmissions or gaps following discharge?
Limitations
  • Rates are crude rather than age-standardised.
  • Community contacts are encounters, not unique patients.
  • Indicators come from different collections and cannot be combined into a measure of demand.
  • Utilisation reflects need, access, capacity, pathways, service models and reporting practice.
  • COVID-19 and population change complicate comparison across the period.
  • The analysis covers three regional PHNs and is not a statewide assessment.
Source and method

Source: AIHW Regional profiles of mental health service activity, dashboard exports retrieved 27 September 2026.

The workflow validates the complete 3-region × 3-indicator × 6-year panel, calculates annual and five-year percentage changes, indexes series to 2018–19 and compares regional ranges. No modelling or significance testing is used.
